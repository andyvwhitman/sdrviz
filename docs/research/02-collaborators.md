# 02 — Collaborators, co-designers and adjacent studios

Researcher: "Collaborator Cartographer" (Stage 1 research, reporting to `gmunk-director`).
Compiled 2026-11-08.

**Scope.** This file covers the people and studios around GMUNK (Bradley G. Munkowitz) on *Tron: Legacy* (2010), *Oblivion* (2013) and *Tron: Ares* (2025), plus a few of his other projects. For each one it gives the role, the signature style or technique, and links. No film stills are embedded. Follow the links to see the work.

**How to read the credit labels.**
- **[Primary]**: from a credit list GMUNK published on gmunk.com, or from the artist's own portfolio.
- **[Secondary]**: from press interviews or articles (Motionographer, ProVideo Coalition, Pushing Pixels, Art of VFX and similar).
- **[Aggregator / uncertain]**: from Metacritic, IMDb, fan wikis or AI-assisted search summaries. Treat these as unconfirmed.
- **Self-reported**: the person's own description of their work. It is not independently verified.

**Spelling note.** Sources spell several names differently. "Jake Sargeant" also appears as Sargent and Sergeant. "Joseph Chan" appears as Joseph Chanimal. One source spells the designer "Toros Köse" ([darbyfui.com](https://www.darbyfui.com/tronares)), while GMUNK uses "Kose". This file uses gmunk.com spellings where they exist.

## 1. Tron: Legacy (2010): the Digital Domain "GFX All-Stars"

GMUNK says he was hired as an independent contractor to build a team. The team worked as an outside unit inside Digital Domain's facility ([Pushing Pixels interview](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/2011/06/01/visual-effects-of-tron-legacy-and-beyond-conversation-with-gmunk.html)). Sources disagree on how much screen time the team made:
- "approximately 10 minutes" of UI sequences and holograms (search summary of [oregonconfluence.com](https://oregonconfluence.com/2011/09/20/byte-by-byte-gmunk-takes-apart-the-vfx-of-tron-legacy/)).
- "about 12 minutes" of holographic content ([gmunk.com/TRON-Board-Room](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Board-Room)).

GMUNK has said he was first approached about the on-set graphics, but that work "got outsourced to a different company" (search summary of [Inventing Interactive, 2011](https://inventinginteractive.com/2011/03/02/interview-gmunk/); I could not open the page directly because its TLS certificate has expired).

## 2. Oblivion (2013): Crater Lake Productions

GMUNK's own credit list gives the production facility as Crater Lake Productions, with Kosinski directing, Steve Gaubs producing and David Feinsilber as assistant producer ([gmunk.com/Oblivion-GFX](https://gmunk.com/Oblivion-GFX)). Unlike *Legacy*, Kosinski was the team's only direct contact ([Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2013/04/19/bradley-g-munkowitz-oblivion-screen-graphics/)).

The brief: "functionality and minimalism while utilizing a bright, unified color palette" that works on both dark and light backgrounds ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/hud-and-gui-graphics-in-oblivion/)).

## 3. Tron: Ares (2025): GMUNK Studio, Inc.

**Scope figures.** Sources give different numbers, and they may be measuring different things:
- GMUNK on X: "over 2 years working on all the computer vision, screen interfaces, hologram design and a wide assortment of concept work for over 250 shots" ([X post](https://x.com/gmunk/status/1980672924253319261)).
- The studio's Computer Vision page: a team of twenty and more than sixty shots for that sequence family ([gmunk.com/TRON-Ares-Computer-Vision](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Ares-Computer-Vision)).
- Jayse Hansen's portfolio: 260+ shots ([jayse.io](https://jayse.io/)) [self-reported].

## Whose techniques matter most for sdrviz and why

sdrviz is a calm, always-on instrument on a Pi or CPU-only box, with SVG chrome and Canvas/WebGL for the spectrum (decisions #2, #4 and #7). Ranked by relevance:

1. **Joseph Chan and the Oblivion Light Table team (Sargeant, Lewandowski, Perry): the Light Table as our Live Ops screen.**
   - The Light Table is the nearest film analogue to sdrviz. It is a multi-pane operator console with a main map tracking assets, a vitals and fuel monitor, a resource monitor, a weather screen and "Rogue Signal" feed windows ([gmunk.com](https://gmunk.com/Oblivion-GFX)).
   - That maps almost one to one onto Map, Feeds health, Stats and Live traffic.
   - Chan built the graphics as loops with start-up and shut-down states for live playback ([Pushing Pixels](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/?p=9032)). sdrviz needs the same: idle loops plus a defined arming and disarming choreography per panel.
   - The brief, "functionality and minimalism … bright, unified color palette" ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/hud-and-gui-graphics-in-oblivion/)), already matches decision #7.
   - Navarro Parker identified the typeface as Blender. The design lead should check its licensing, or pick an open-licence equivalent.

2. **Jayse Hansen: legibility rules for something left open for hours.**
   - His rules translate directly into spec constraints: mostly black negative space, every text element tied to real context, and legibility even at micro-text sizes ([Pushing Pixels](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/?p=7447)).
   - His habit of interviewing real operators suggests reviewing the Live Ops layout against how a railfan or dispatcher actually listens.
   - The Figma-to-After-Effects workflow ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1IHZ6LpSHik)) suggests a sensible pipeline for the mockup stage: static frames first, then motion.

3. **Josh Nimoy and Keith Pasko: code art and truthful data.**
   - Their openFrameworks apps generated linework procedurally, as in "Tronlines" and the "Egyptian" line algorithm that drove bracket animations ([Disc Game](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Disc-Game)). That is exactly the mode sdrviz runs in: visuals computed live from data, not pre-rendered.
   - Nimoy insisted on real `ps | grep` and `kill` commands ([Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/18933/josh-nimoy-on-the-secrets-behind-the-special-effects-in-tron-legacy-18933)). That supports showing *real* values in sdrviz: frequencies, dBFS, SNR, sample rate, AAR channel numbers. Use no fake greeble.
   - Caveat: Lewandowski warned that standalone code art was unreliable in production ([Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2011/06/06/tron-legacy-in-depth-with-david-lewandowski/)). For us that argues for simple, deterministic SVG and Canvas renderers rather than heavy generative engines on a Pi.

4. **Darby Faccinto and the Ares square-packing team (Cashman, Swaab): one shared logic and motion that serves the content.**
   - "Scan, track, connect, populate, animate", all driven by one logic ([gmunk.com](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Ares-Computer-Vision)), is a good model for a single motion grammar shared by every sdrviz panel. For example, a new transmission is detected, locked, connected to its feed and channel, filled in with a transcript, and then decays.
   - Faccinto's split between a crisp HUD and a soft, motion-blurred Grid ([darbyfui.com](https://www.darbyfui.com/tronares)) suggests two layers for us: sharp SVG chrome over a softer, glowing waterfall.
   - "Directive based … nothing decorative" matches the director's "every element must mean something".
   - Ares' urgent red-orange palette is *not* our base. It is useful only as reference for the amber "transmitting" and red "fault" states.

5. **Kosinski as reductive editor, and Prologue / Danny Yount for typography.**
   - The recurring pattern across sources is that the director and the "upper echelons" simplified the designs: cleaner titles, a simplified globe, minimalist glass ([Opening Titles](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Opening-Titles), [Globe](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Rectifier-Globe), [Throne Room](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Throne-Room)). Reviews should apply that subtractive pass.
   - Prologue's *Ares* problem, legible type over a busy, flashing field ([prologue.com](https://prologue.com/portfolio-item/tronares/)), is the same problem as transcript text over an active waterfall.

**Lower relevance for sdrviz:** Daniel Simon (vehicles), the Tron concept artists and ILM's Grid environments set the world, but they don't inform the interface. Pixomondo's glass smudges and reflections are photoreal effects that a lightweight web app should avoid.
